# Harborline

Port-to-door freight visibility. Sells to logistics directors and supply-chain
planners at mid-market importers. Tone is exact and unhurried — the product's
promise is that nothing is a surprise, so the brand never shouts.

Last reviewed: 2026-08-04

## Palette

- primary: #0B3A45
- secondary: #1F7A8C
- accent: #00A878
- surface: #FFFFFF
- ink: #0A1416
- muted: #6E8A91

## Type

- heading: Barlow
- body: Barlow

## Type scale

- h1: 52px
- h2: 32px
- h3: 21px
- body: 16px
- caption: 12px

## Shape

- border-radius: 4px
- Every corner is 4px — cards, image containers and buttons alike. Nothing is
  pill-shaped and nothing is a perfect square.

## Applying it

Headlines are Barlow 700 at h1, set tight and sentence case. Never all caps in a
headline. The eyebrow above a headline is Barlow 600 at caption size, letterspaced,
in muted — not in the accent. Body copy is Barlow 400.

The ground is either surface white or a solid fill of primary. There is no
gradient anywhere in this brand. On the primary ground, headline and body are
surface white and the reverse logo is used.

The accent is a status colour before it is a brand colour: it means "on time",
so it carries the call to action and nothing else. A primary button is a solid
fill of #00A878 with ink label text at Barlow 600. A secondary button is a 1px
#1F7A8C outline with the label in secondary. Never use the accent as a
background for a region larger than a button, and never tint it.

One structural device is allowed and encouraged: a single 2px horizontal rule in
secondary, full-bleed, separating the headline block from the supporting copy. It
reads as a shipping lane. Do not stack two of them.

Photography is container yards, quay cranes and vessel decks in flat daylight.
Never a stock boardroom, never a person at a laptop, never an aerial map render.
